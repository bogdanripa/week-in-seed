# The Week in Seed: Founders Who Already Sold One Fintech Raised the Biggest Checks to Build the Next

### Two operators with prior exits pulled in $115M combined to rebuild financial infrastructure from scratch, while smaller checks chased critical minerals out of mining waste and watched wildfires from geostationary orbit.

It was a week where the size of the check tracked the size of the founder's resume, not the size of the idea. The two largest seed rounds went to people rebuilding infrastructure they'd already built once before, while everything else — mining waste, orbital sensors — stayed in single-digit millions and pre-revenue.

## The biggest seed checks went to founders rebuilding infrastructure they'd already sold once

Investors wrote two of the week's largest checks to a specific credential: not a novel product idea, but a founder who has already built and sold financial infrastructure, now doing it again with AI as the foundation rather than a bolt-on. That's a narrower bet than it looks. Financial infrastructure — bank cores, advisory relationships — is expensive to get wrong in ways that show up years later as compliance failures or client churn, so underwriting execution risk by pattern-matching on "has done this before, successfully" is a rational shortcut when the technology itself (AI models good enough to run advisory or agentic workflows) has only recently become viable. It also reflects a shared structural bet: in both cases, the existing stack is old enough — legacy bank cores built for a pre-API era in one case, a near-total absence of real advisory access below the private-banking tier in the other — that a greenfield AI-native rebuild now looks cheaper than retrofitting.

This extends a pattern flagged in this newsletter three weeks ago, when a repeat-founder AI voice startup reopened its seed round at $100M. The difference this time is scale of ambition: instead of a single vertical product, both of this week's mega-checks are aimed at the infrastructure layer itself — the system everything else in the vertical has to run on top of. Checks north of $25M at the seed stage remain rare enough that landing two in the same week, both from repeat fintech operators, both explicitly framed as "AI-native from the ground up" rather than "AI added to what exists," is worth treating as a pattern rather than a coincidence.

### [Decade](https://decade.com) — $85M seed (led by Greenoaks, Benchmark, and Diffusion)
- **Problem:** only about a third of Brazilians hold any financial investments, and the rest have no equivalent of a private banker — retail investors get self-serve trading apps at best and fee-heavy brokers at worst, with nothing that looks like an actual advisory relationship.
- **Product:** pairs every client with a human advisor backed by proprietary AI that continuously analyzes spending, holdings, and market activity across three functions — "Protect" (fee, tax, and concentration-risk cleanup), "Prepare" (goal-based planning), "Prosper" (long-term investing) — with the human layer reachable via WhatsApp and video and the AI layer live in a consolidated dashboard.
- **Customer:** Brazilian (and broader Latin American) earners in the emerging-affluent bracket who want advisory-level attention historically reserved for private-bank clients — the trigger is income outpacing access to real financial guidance.
- **Stage:** $85M seed led by Greenoaks, Benchmark and Diffusion — the largest seed round ever raised by a Latin American startup; founded by former Nubank CTO Vitor Olivier and Hyperplane founder Felipe Meneses, with an advisory bench drawn from Blackstone and Coatue ([announcement](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260804082552/en/Decade-Raises-$85M-in-Latin-Americas-Largest-Seed-Round-to-Create-a-New-Generation-of-Millionaires-with-AI)).
- **TAM:** AI-powered wealth-management software specifically is a narrow ~$1.8B–$2B category today, but the broader AI-in-wealth-management market is estimated at $20.8B in 2024, heading toward $129.6B by 2034 (Future Market Insights, InsightAce Analytic) — a wide range that mostly reflects how much adjacent advisory software gets counted in.
- **Moat:** a proprietary model trained on continuous client financial data, paired with a staffed human advisory bench, is a real cost-to-replicate barrier today — but the underwriting question is whether "advisor plus AI" beats a pure-AI robo-product on price once AI quality commoditizes further, especially if Nubank itself (where both founders came from) ships a comparable AI advisory layer to its existing base.

### [Maximum](https://maximum.com) — $30M seed (led by CRV)
- **Problem:** more than 70% of the roughly 5,000 U.S. banks still run on legacy core systems built decades ago that were never designed to host AI agents or support real-time product iteration — bolting AI onto that stack means working around the system of record, not through it.
- **Product:** an AI-native core operating system for banks, with AI integrated at the foundational layer rather than layered on top, letting a bank build and deploy custom agents for operational workflows, get real-time visibility into customer activity, and ship new financial products without first ripping out its legacy core.
- **Customer:** U.S. community and regional banks stuck on decades-old core providers, whose trigger is wanting agentic AI capability their current core architecturally can't support.
- **Stage:** $30M seed led by CRV, with Pear VC, Restive, Plug and Play Ventures and Anthemis participating; founded by three-time fintech founder Randy Fernando, who previously sold Vault to Acorns in 2017 and Power to Marqeta in 2023 ([announcement](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/08/03/3337824/0/en/maximum-raises-30-million-to-build-the-ai-native-operating-system-for-banks.html)).
- **TAM:** core banking software runs roughly $14B–$20B today, projected to $36B–$84B by the mid-2030s depending on the estimate (Precedence Research, Fortune Business Insights) — Maximum is chasing the modernization slice of an unusually sticky, slow-to-switch market.
- **Moat:** replacing a bank's system of record is one of the highest-switching-cost sales in software, which cuts both ways — a won customer is very hard to lose, but getting there against entrenched vendors like Fiserv and FIS, with decades of compliance relationships, is a sales cycle long enough to test any seed-stage runway.

## Critical minerals got mined twice this week — once from the ground, once from the waste pile

Domestic critical-mineral supply is a stated policy priority across the U.S. and its allies, but a new mine takes the better part of a decade to permit and produce. The fastest path to new supply this week's money is chasing isn't a new mine at all — it's better economics on volumes that already exist: waste already sitting in stockpiles, or existing underground operations pushed to run safer and more productively without opening a new pit. Both bets share a moat problem worth watching. The underlying technology — hydrometallurgical chemistry in one case, thermal-vision perception in the other — is difficult to build well but not obviously impossible for a well-capitalized rival to replicate. That means the real defensibility is less about the science and more about who signs exclusive processing or data-sharing agreements with the handful of major mine operators and waste generators first, before the majors decide to build the capability in-house.

### [FAST Metals](https://www.fastmetalsinc.com) — $4.3M pre-seed (led by New Climate Ventures)
- **Problem:** alumina refining has left more than 4 billion tons of toxic "red mud" waste in stockpiles worldwide, most of it simply sitting there, even though it contains critical minerals — scandium, titanium, gallium, rare earths — that manufacturers and defense buyers currently import or mine from scratch.
- **Product:** a modular hydrometallurgical process that extracts iron, aluminum, scandium, titanium and rare-earth elements from red mud and other iron-bearing mine waste on-site, without generating new waste of its own.
- **Customer:** alumina refiners and mining-waste operators sitting on red-mud stockpiles who want a liability turned into a revenue line, plus downstream buyers — defense contractors and clean-energy manufacturers — who need scandium, titanium and rare-earth supply that doesn't run through China.
- **Stage:** $4.3M pre-seed led by New Climate Ventures, with Azolla Ventures, Humba Ventures, Swiss family office Astor Swiss, and Rio Tinto's mining-tech accelerator with Founders Factory; the company just signed its first commercial deal, processing a ton of feedstock per week at Metalox Mineral Corporation's Florida site ([announcement](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/fast-metals-raises-4-3-million-and-commercializes-critical-mineral-recovery-technology-302837149.html)).
- **TAM:** red mud stockpiles are estimated to hold $3T–$4T in recoverable metal value globally, while the standalone rare-earth metals market is a much narrower $7.2B in 2025, heading to $12.6B by 2035 (ResearchNester) — the trillion-dollar figure is a resource estimate, not a realizable market size, and should be read as a ceiling rather than a forecast.
- **Moat:** being first to a signed offtake and processing deal with an operator like Metalox is a real distribution edge in a market where feedstock access, not chemistry alone, gates who can scale — but the core hydrometallurgical process isn't obviously unique, and better-funded rivals, or the mining majors themselves, several of which already invest in this space, could replicate it once the unit economics are proven out.

### [LoopX](https://loopx.ai) — $4.05M seed (led by BDC Seed Venture Fund and Orion Industrial Ventures)
- **Problem:** underground and open-pit mining conditions — dust, variable lighting, unpredictable terrain — routinely defeat the camera-based perception systems that autonomous and driver-assist vehicles rely on everywhere else, keeping collision risk and productivity loss high in exactly the environments where an incident is most costly.
- **Product:** a thermal-vision-plus-LiDAR physical-AI platform with three modules already deployed — a collision avoidance system, LoadSight for payload and productivity monitoring, and BoomGuard for boom-equipment safety — running on edge compute so it keeps working without reliable connectivity underground.
- **Customer:** underground mining operators and equipment contractors running mixed autonomous and human-driven fleets, currently live at eight active mine sites; the trigger is a near-miss or incident that exposes camera-based systems' blind spots in dust or darkness.
- **Stage:** $4.05M seed led by BDC Seed Venture Fund and Orion Industrial Ventures, with engineering firm Hatch participating; the company also just signed a three-year R&D partnership with Vale Base Metals ([announcement](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260804630169/en/LoopX-Raises-$4.05M-Seed-Round-Led-by-BDC-Seed-Venture-Fund-and-Orion-Industrial-Ventures-with-Participation-from-Hatch)).
- **TAM:** mining collision-avoidance software alone is estimated at $1.2B–$3.4B today, heading to $2.5B–$9.1B by the early-to-mid 2030s across differing estimates (Vantage Market Research, Verified Market Reports) — a small category, though LoopX is also expanding into adjacent productivity-monitoring revenue.
- **Moat:** real deployment data from eight live mine sites, in dust and darkness conditions competitors can't easily replicate, is a genuine data edge if it compounds — but equipment OEMs like Caterpillar and Komatsu building perception directly into new machines is the longer-term threat that could make third-party retrofits like LoopX's less necessary over time.

## A wildfire sensor doubles as a missile-warning system, and that's the business model

Commercial space companies increasingly design a single sensor payload to serve civil and defense customers at once, because building and launching satellites is capital-intensive enough that a single-customer business case rarely pencils out at the pre-seed stage. Government contracts are a patient, high-margin anchor once won, but slow to close; civil agencies and insurers move faster and provide the recurring commercial revenue that keeps a company alive in the meantime. Pairing the two — one sensor, two very different customer bases — is becoming a repeatable formula for early-stage space companies rather than an opportunistic exception, and this week's round is a clean example of the pattern.

### [Orbital Sentry](https://www.orbitalsentry.ai) — pre-seed, amount not disclosed (led by Ridgeline)
- **Problem:** existing wildfire detection relies on satellites that pass over a given patch of ground only periodically, or on ground-based spotting — both too slow to catch a fire in its first minutes, when it's still cheap to contain.
- **Product:** geosynchronous satellites carrying persistent infrared sensors that hold a fixed view of the same region continuously, streaming near-real-time fire detection and perimeter tracking instead of periodic snapshots — the same sensing architecture doubles as a missile-launch detection and battlespace-awareness feed for defense customers.
- **Customer:** first responders, utilities, insurers and government wildfire agencies across the Western U.S. at launch, expanding toward defense and critical-infrastructure monitoring customers as the constellation grows — the trigger is any organization for which minutes of earlier warning materially change the outcome.
- **Stage:** pre-seed led by Ridgeline, with Superorganism and Hustle Fund participating; amount not disclosed. Founded by Dr. Mark Keremedjiev, the company is using the round to build its first imaging sensor and expand government and commercial partnerships ([announcement](https://pulse2.com/orbital-sentry-raises-pre-seed-funding-for-geosynchronous-infrared-imaging-platform/)).
- **TAM:** wildfire detection systems are roughly $2.1B–$2.13B today, projected to $6.3B–$7.8B by 2033 across estimates, while satellite-based Earth observation broadly runs about $4.3B in 2025 toward $9.2B by 2035 (Spherical Insights) — Orbital Sentry's dual-use defense revenue, missile warning and battlespace awareness, sits in a separate government-budget line not captured in either civil figure.
- **Moat:** continuous geosynchronous coverage is a real technical differentiator against the low-Earth-orbit satellites most competitors fly, and a dual civil-defense customer base diversifies revenue — but building and launching a GEO constellation costs well beyond what pre-seed money covers, so near-term survival depends on government interest converting into contracts before the company needs a much larger round.

## What to watch next week

This week's five rounds skewed almost entirely toward the Americas — Brazil, Miami, Washington D.C., Ontario — with a single Saudi outlier not featured here and zero rounds out of continental Europe or Asia among the deals that made the cut; worth noting as a gap rather than something to correct for. Watch whether Decade's advisor-plus-AI model holds its price premium as Nubank and other LatAm incumbents ship their own AI advisory layers; whether Maximum can land a marquee regional-bank customer to prove the switching-cost thesis before a longer sales cycle burns through $30M; and whether FAST Metals' one-ton-a-week Metalox pilot scales into a real offtake volume before a mining major decides to build red-mud recovery in-house.
