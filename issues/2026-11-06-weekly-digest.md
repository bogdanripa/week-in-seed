# The Week in Seed: Two Ways to Beat an Incumbent — Charge by the Result, or Build It for a Fraction of the Price

### A radar pitched at a fraction of incumbent prices, an unmanned undersea freighter, an audit agent with a guarantee and a fixed-fee law firm

A week of challengers who each attacked one specific thing the incumbent charges too much for.

## Defense is being rebuilt by alumni of the primes, aimed at what the primes price out

The defense seed pattern this year has been founders who spent careers inside the large contractors and consultancies, left, and now sell against them. The pitch is rarely "better technology." It is a different cost structure, or a bet that the next conflict turns on logistics and sensing that the legacy procurement stack under-serves. Cheap drones changed the economics of attack faster than they changed the economics of detection and resupply, and that mismatch is where seed money is going.

We have covered defense in several issues, so the useful question is what is new. This week's rounds are both about the unglamorous layer: knowing something is coming, and moving mass to where it is needed without being seen. Neither is a weapon. That keeps them out of the most contested procurement lanes, and it also means the buyer is a ministry or a navy, with timelines that are measured in years.

The underwriting problem is the same across the group. Technical claims are the company's own, customers are unnamed, and the first real test is a government deciding to buy. Investors are paying today for access to a market whose demand is asserted, not yet contracted.

### [Gallium Defense](https://gallium-defense.com) — €7.1M seed (led by Keen Venture Partners and 360 Capital)
- **Problem:** Small FPV drones threaten airports, ports, energy sites and military assets, but good radar costs millions of euros while cheap detectors see only a few kilometres. Gallium's own framing: meet 80% of the need at 20% of the price.
- **Product:** GDS-10, a software-defined X-band AESA 3D radar with edge AI, in a civil version and a defense version (GDS-10-M). The company says it cut antenna unit cost more than tenfold by adapting telecom-grade gallium components, and that two antennas have detected Class 1 drones at 10 km.
- **Customer:** Critical-infrastructure operators (airports, ports, energy) and armed forces that need wide coverage and can afford several units rather than one flagship sensor.
- **Stage:** Seed, announced on exit from stealth; Omnes Capital and Polytechnique Ventures also participated. No revenue; commercial orders open in Q1 2027; five potential MOUs under discussion, none final; about 15 staff. The 10 km result came from controlled tests in good weather against known targets ([announcement](https://tech.eu/2026/11/02/gallium-defense-raises-eur71m-to-make-drone-detection-cheaper-and-scalable//)).
- **TAM:** Analyst estimates for counter-drone systems by 2030 range from about $9B to $20B depending on how the market is defined; the narrowest definitions are far lower. Treat as directional estimates only.
- **Moat:** Cost is the claim, and cost advantages in hardware erode once a prime decides to compete. The durable parts would be NATO-standard interoperability and field feedback loops, but both are copyable. The question is whether performance in rain and under jamming holds up, which the founders admit remains to be tested.

### [Corridian](https://www.corridian.com) — $38M seed (led by Point72 Ventures)
- **Problem:** Ships and aircraft are increasingly easy to find and hard to defend, and existing autonomous underwater vehicles and submarines can't move large, dense cargo.
- **Product:** Ultra-large autonomous underwater vehicles carrying ISO-standard containerized payloads thousands of nautical miles without a crew, which the company calls "stealth logistics." First vehicle to be unveiled in 2027; specifications, including how it launches, were not shared.
- **Customer:** Navies and defense ministries planning for contested logistics, plus intelligence and payload-delivery missions.
- **Stage:** Seed, founded September 2025, emerged from stealth; Balerion Space Ventures, Harpoon Ventures, DCVC and Marlinspike also in. The founders previously led a large undersea autonomy portfolio at General Dynamics, with programs in service with the U.S. Navy and Royal Australian Navy; former CNO Lisa Franchetti is senior military adviser. No customer or contract named ([announcement](https://thenextweb.com/news/corridian-38m-seed-point72-underwater-drones-stealth-logistics)).
- **TAM:** Not disclosed in the coverage, and I found no credible sizing for undersea cargo; the market is effectively a handful of government programs.
- **Moat:** Team pedigree and security clearances are the asset today; the hardware does not exist yet. The underwriting question is whether a Navy that has expressed interest in larger vehicles will buy from a seed-stage company instead of its incumbent shipbuilders.

## AI services firms are being priced on the result, not the hour

Licensed work has come up in this newsletter before, most recently the rebuild of trades around the signature. This week adds a commercial angle: pricing. Billable hours reward slow work, so an AI-heavy firm that charges by the hour passes its efficiency gains to the client and keeps the margin problem. The startups here instead sell a fixed fee or a guarantee, which turns speed and accuracy into the product.

That is a harder business than software. A guarantee or a flat fee puts the vendor on the hook for errors, and the reputation risk of one bad deliverable lands on a firm that has to keep its professional sign-off. The upside is that the vendor can capture the saving instead of handing it to the client in lower hours.

Expect incumbents to respond in two ways: adopt the same tools inside their own firms, and lean on the sign-off they already hold. The challengers' advantage is that they are built around the new unit of work from day one, and the open question is whether that gap persists once a large firm copies the pricing.

### [Clark](https://getclark.com/) — $11M seed (led by Touring Capital)
- **Problem:** Audit engagements are labor-heavy, chasing documents and preparing workpapers, and every hour of that work is billed by the hour, so efficiency gains don't help firms that sell time.
- **Product:** "Clerks," autonomous AI audit employees that chase client documents and prepare workpapers while licensed CPAs keep the final opinion. Clark's site describes drafts with sources attached and traceable figures, and it advertises a peer-review guarantee; the CEO also describes a "100% accuracy guarantee" whose terms are not published.
- **Customer:** Mid-sized CPA firms that audit clients and are limited by how many staff they can field per busy season.
- **Stage:** Seed bringing total funding to $16M, with Marathon Management Partners, Rerail and Sofeon Ventures also in. The company, formerly uiAgent, says more than 35 CPA firms use it, including CliftonLarsonAllen and Aprio; those counts are company claims ([announcement](https://www.corporatecomplianceinsights.com/audit-ai-startup-clark-raises-11m-seed-round/)).
- **TAM:** Published estimates for auditing services run from roughly $70B to about $300B depending on whether the scope is statutory audit only or includes internal, IT and advisory work; the software slice is a small fraction of that.
- **Moat:** Embedded workflows and firm-specific methodology help, and the guarantee is a differentiator only if it is backed with real money. Competition exists, and no error-rate data has been shown.

### [Athena](https://www.athena.lawyer/) — $2.5M pre-seed (led by a16z Speedrun)
- **Problem:** Commercial real estate deals are priced in billable hours, so the same diligence and drafting takes lawyer time that the client can't forecast.
- **Product:** An AI-native legal service for commercial real estate acquisitions, sales and financing: agents draft and review purchase and loan documents and handle diligence, while attorneys at an affiliated law firm approve the deliverables. Pricing is a fixed fee per transaction, typically 30–50% below standard rates, per the company.
- **Customer:** Real estate owners and operators doing repeat deals, plus law firms; 22 law firms have signed up as clients and Mox Living is a named customer.
- **Stage:** Pre-seed, with the service launched about two months before announcement; the company says agents cut review times by 70% on average. Founders are ex-Legora; these are company claims ([announcement](https://siliconangle.com/2026/11/03/startup-athena-raises-2-5m-in-pre-seed-funding-to-revamp-the-revenue-model-for-legal-ai/)).
- **TAM:** Not disclosed in the coverage.
- **Moat:** Thin on technology and thicker on regulation: the affiliated-firm structure is what lets AI-priced legal work be sold at all. The question is whether the speed gain survives a large firm adopting the same tools.

## What to watch next week

- Whether Gallium converts any of its five MOU conversations into a public order, and whether it tests in poor weather.
- Whether Clark publishes the terms of its guarantee; without them the headline claim can't be underwritten.
- Whether another AI-native service startup raises at seed with a fixed-fee or guarantee model, which would confirm this is a pricing shift and not one pitch.
